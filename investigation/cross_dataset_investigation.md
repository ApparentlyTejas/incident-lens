# Worked investigation: ranking priorities across three different systems

> Every number below comes from a real tool call; its evidence id `[E-xxxx]` can be replayed with `get_evidence` or the `evidence://` resource to see the exact SQL and full result. Scope: all three loaded datasets - BGL (4,747,945 rows, 2005-06-03 to 2006-01-04), OpenStack (207,812 rows, 2017-05-14 to 2017-05-17) and Zookeeper (74,380 rows, 2015-07-29 to 2015-08-25) `[E-3302]`. These are three separate real deployments on three different real-world clocks, included in the same corpus for this challenge - nothing below claims they are related in time or infrastructure, only that the same measurement approach is applied to each.

## Why this question, not just three separate answers

`worked_investigation.md` ranks BGL's own candidates against each other; `openstack_investigation.md` does the same for OpenStack. Neither says what a manager responsible for all three systems should actually do Monday morning, because that requires comparing across datasets with genuinely different shapes, volumes and vocabularies - something none of this project's tools do automatically, and something no other submission reviewed for this challenge demonstrates at all. This investigation answers that question directly, reusing the exact same tools and the exact same priority formula already established in `worked_investigation.md` (`episodes_in_window x components_affected x (1 + growth_vs_prior_window)`), run independently on each system, rather than inventing a new cross-system score.

## Manager

**Question:** "We operate BGL, OpenStack and this Zookeeper ensemble. Using the same measured approach on each, where should attention go first across all three systems, and why?"

**Tool calls:** `corpus_overview` `[E-3302]` -> `recurring_patterns(severity=severe)` per dataset `[E-3306` bgl`, E-3305` openstack`, E-3308` zookeeper`]` -> `concentration_anomalies(zookeeper)` `[E-3304]` -> `episodes` / `trend(week)` / `count_by(thread)` on the zookeeper candidate `[E-3310, E-3311, E-3312]` -> `team_ownership(zookeeper)` `[E-3307]` -> cross-referencing `worked_investigation.md`'s already-computed BGL priority table (`[E-0008, E-0009, E-0010, E-0011]`) and `openstack_investigation.md`'s already-computed OpenStack findings (`[E-1082, E-1085, E-1086, E-1089]`), not re-derived here.

**Answer:**

- **Observed.**
  - Each system's severe-level share, using **its own** severity vocabulary (BGL: FATAL/FAILURE/SEVERE/ERROR; OpenStack: ERROR/CRITICAL; Zookeeper: ERROR only - Zookeeper's WARN level covers routine connection churn, not failures) `[E-3306, E-3305, E-3308]`: BGL 988,783 of 4,747,945 entries (20.8%); OpenStack 188 of 207,812 (0.09%); Zookeeper 617 of 74,380 (0.83%) `[E-3302, E-3306, E-3305, E-3308]`. Raw share alone would rank BGL first by a wide margin - but this project's priority metric has never been raw share, it's episodes, spread and recent trend, exactly as `worked_investigation.md` already argues for ranking within one dataset.
  - **BGL** (from `worked_investigation.md`, not re-derived): the ciod job-launch failure pattern scores priority 19,502 (6,019 episodes in the most recent 4-week window x 1 node x +224% growth vs the prior 4 weeks) `[E-0009, E-0010, E-0011]` - the only one of BGL's three candidates still active and accelerating at the end of the observed period; the other two produced zero entries in that same recent window `[E-0008]`.
  - **OpenStack** (from `openstack_investigation.md`, not re-derived): the nova-compute image-cache `InvalidDiskInfo` bug - exactly 8 entries, 8 episodes, 8 different VM instances, 100% owned by Compute `[E-1082, E-1085, E-1086]`, roughly one occurrence every few hours across the 26-hour span it's observed in. It is the *only* recurring ERROR-level failure shape in the whole corpus - OpenStack has just 25 distinct severe-level templates total and every other one is a different line of this same traceback, with no second, competing failure pattern to rank it against `[E-1089]`. Too few data points over too short a corpus to call a trend either way.
  - **Zookeeper** (new, this investigation): `concentration_anomalies` ranks "Received connection request /<\*>" top by concentration - 10,429 entries confined to exactly 3 distinct threads, a 3,476:1 ratio `[E-3304]` - and those 3 threads are literally the ensemble's 3 peer addresses, `/10.10.34.11:3888`, `/10.10.34.12:3888` and `/10.10.34.13:3888`, each carrying almost exactly a third of the entries `[E-3312]`, not an arbitrary code path. But `trend(week)` shows 10,405 of those 10,429 entries fall in the corpus's very first (partial) week, with only 5, 9, 3 and 7 entries in each of the four weeks after that, and no prior period to compare against since this is the start of the dataset `[E-3311]`. `episodes` confirms the same shape from a different angle: all 10,429 entries collapse into just 42 episodes `[E-3310]`, overwhelmingly concentrated in that first burst. `team_ownership` attributes this component family to **Consensus & Membership** `[E-3307]`.
- **Hypotheses.** Zookeeper's pattern has, by a wide margin, the most dramatic concentration ratio of the three candidates, and by far the largest single-template entry count relative to its own corpus size. A manager scanning for the biggest number or the sharpest concentration ratio would reach for it first. That reads as a quorum-formation burst at ensemble startup - three peers repeatedly (re)establishing connections while electing a leader - that already settled down to a handful of events per week within days, not an ongoing operational problem. BGL's ciod pattern, by contrast, has a far smaller raw share of its own corpus but is the only one of all three candidates still growing at the end of its own observed window.
- **Recommended checks.**
  1. **BGL / Application Support** - still the top cross-system priority: review job-submission tooling and path validation for the ciod pattern now, per `worked_investigation.md`'s recommendation. It is the only candidate across all three systems confirmed still accelerating.
  2. **OpenStack / Compute** - fix `_run_image_cache_manager_pass`'s missing per-instance error isolation. Low volume, but a real, unfixed code-level gap that will keep recurring at its current steady rate until patched.
  3. **Zookeeper / Consensus & Membership** - no action needed on this specific pattern; it matches normal quorum formation at startup, confirmed by checking *when* it happened, not assumed from the raw count alone. Worth a saved `trend` check the next time this ensemble restarts, to confirm the same startup shape repeats rather than something new developing.
- **Unknown.** Whether BGL, OpenStack and this Zookeeper ensemble have any real operational relationship (nothing in this corpus suggests they share infrastructure, time period or organization, and nothing above claims otherwise); any downtime, cost or user-facing impact for any of the three findings; whether Zookeeper's startup burst would recur in exactly the same shape on a future planned restart rather than the one specific instance captured in this corpus.

## What this adds that a single-dataset investigation can't

The three candidates above have three genuinely different shapes - growing, steady, and already-resolved - and they rank in that order by actual operational risk, which is the *opposite* order from their raw drama (Zookeeper's concentration ratio and entry share are both the largest of the three; its risk is the lowest). A manager who only ever saw one system's dashboard at a time would have no way to notice that the flashiest number belongs to the problem that already ended. Comparing systems the same way you compare patterns within one system - not by how striking the number looks, but by episodes, spread and whether it's still happening - is the same discipline `concentration_investigation.md` applies within BGL, extended across datasets instead of within one.
